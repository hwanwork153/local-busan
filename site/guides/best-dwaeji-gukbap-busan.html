The best dwaeji gukbap in Busan: Yangsan Wang Dwaeji Gukbap

Every Busan local has a gukbap place they swear by. This is ours. A no-frills neighborhood spot in Jaesong-dong where you'll hear nothing but Busan dialect — and where the set meal comes with a side dish and a free cup of makgeolli you won't get anywhere else.

HHyunseo · Busan local for 24 years · Updated November 18, 2026
양산왕돼지국밥
Quick answer

If you only eat dwaeji gukbap once in Busan, go to Yangsan Wang Dwaeji Gukbap (양산왕돼지국밥) in Jaesong-dong, Haeundae-gu, and order the suyuk baekban (pork soup set with a plate of boiled pork, ₩13,000) — it comes with a sweet homemade gajami sikhae side that is the real highlight. Grab your free cup of makgeolli from the self-serve bar. Open daily from 7am.

Why it's our #1

Most of Busan's famous gukbap streets are full of visitors. This place isn't. It sits in a residential part of Haeundae, a short ride from Centum City, and the tables are filled with locals: workers on a lunch break, families on the weekend, people coming in for an early breakfast.

The broth is clean and well balanced rather than heavy, the pork is tender, and nothing about it tries to impress tourists. It's simply the bowl we keep coming back to.

What to order: suyuk baekban

Suyuk baekban (수육백반) is the reason to come. You get a bowl of pork soup with rice plus a separate plate of sliced boiled pork, along with side dishes. It's the dish this restaurant is known for, and it's the one we order every time.

The secret weapon is the gajami sikhae (가자미식해). The set comes with this homemade side dish of fermented flounder — sweet, a little tangy, and the perfect contrast to the rich pork. Wrap a slice of pork with a bit of it and you'll understand why locals rave about it. It's the reason we order the baekban set instead of plain gukbap.

Free makgeolli (yes, really)

There's a self-serve bar with country-style makgeolli (Korean rice wine). Each guest can pour one free cup. It's light, a little fizzy, and exactly what a bowl of hot gukbap needs.

How to eat dwaeji gukbap like a local

The soup arrives lightly seasoned on purpose — you finish it at the table. Add a little saeujeot (salted shrimp) for saltiness, a spoonful of buchu (seasoned chives) for freshness, and the red dadaegi paste if you like heat. Taste, adjust, repeat. Dip the boiled pork in saeujeot too.
